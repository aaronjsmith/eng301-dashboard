ENG 201 Core Competency Throughput Dashboard

Process map of the user journey — from opening the dashboard to acting on what the data shows. Written for college leaders, chairs, and faculty. No technical background required.

Companion to the System Design Document · replaces scattered spreadsheets with one shared, visual source of truth

Open / load Role path View & explore Filter / focus Insight Act / share
What this replaces: Today, leaders pull pass-rate data from separate spreadsheets whenever a question comes up. The data is not standardized, so decisions often rest on gut feeling. What you get: one interactive dashboard that turns the college’s student-level course tables into clear KPIs, risk alerts, and equity views — so tutoring and outreach go to the students who need them most.
1 Open the dashboard “Where do I start?”
Launch the dashboard
Opens in the browser — no new software to install. Works with the tools the college already uses.
→
Data loads automatically
Validated student records for ENG 101, ENG 201, MAT 110, and MAT 252 appear as one shared dataset.
Same numbers for every user
→
Ready to explore
Headline measures, charts, and filters appear together. Refresh or reload when new semester data arrives.
2 Choose your role path “What am I allowed — and meant — to see?”
One product, three lenses
Senior leader / admin
Big-picture view across all four courses. Spot which gateway courses trail and where equity gaps raise alerts.
Program chair
Cross-course and section awareness. Drill into one course and compare student groups to target support.
Faculty
Own sections only. Compare “my students” against the course average without seeing other instructors’ rosters.
3 Pick the view that matches your question “How are we doing — overall, by course, or by equity?”
Overview
Pass rates for all four courses. Makes the central finding visible: 100-level gateway courses sit below their 200-level successors.
Course detail
One course at a time. Compare paired student groups (1st-gen, Pell, English background, enrollment, residency) against the course rate.
Equity / risk
Gender and other gaps ranked by size. Alerts fire when a gap crosses the five-point threshold (e.g., ENG 101).
Presets & highlights
Ready-made KPI/KRI cards and flagged patterns so leaders do not start from a blank spreadsheet.
4 Narrow with filters “Show me first-generation students in ENG 201.”
Global filter bar
Course, session, major, gender, age band, 1st-gen, Pell, English background, residency, enrollment intensity.
→
Charts & KPIs update live
Every panel uses the same filtered population — no conflicting numbers between tabs or charts.
One source of truth
→
Privacy guardrails
Very small groups are suppressed so the dashboard stays useful without exposing individual students.
5 Read the insight “Where should support go?”
Headline KPIs
Pass rate by course and by student group — progress toward goals, shown the same way for everyone.
KRIs & alerts
Early warnings when a gap or risk measure crosses its threshold (e.g., gender gap > 5 points).
Highlights & flags
Surfaces the patterns worth acting on — such as first-generation students in ENG 201 trailing peers by ~12 points.
Drill deeper
From Overview → one course → groups within it. The loop lets leaders keep asking until they have an answer.
6 Share and act “How do I take this into a meeting?”
Export current view
Capture the dashboard exactly as filtered — for budget asks, chair meetings, or student-success huddles.
→
Print a pertinent report
A clean, stamped report of the measures that matter for that decision.
→
Target support
Fund tutoring, plan outreach, or schedule interventions where the evidence points — then reopen next term to see if numbers moved.
Evidence over gut feeling
★ How leaders use it Two minutes to a focused ask

Dean of Academics — tutoring budget

Opens Overview and sees ENG 101 and MAT 110 trail the other courses. Switches to Equity and finds ENG 101 flagged with an 11-point gender gap.

→ Focused request: fund tutoring for 100-level gateways, with outreach to the students falling behind in ENG 101.

English chair — who struggles in ENG 201?

Opens Course Detail, selects ENG 201, and sees first-generation students passing at 77.1% — about twelve points below peers.

→ Shares the view with student success; plans early outreach to first-generation students next semester.
